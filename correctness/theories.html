Theories

The theory layer is governed by eight laws. L-theories-1 characterizes well-formed signatures, and L-theories-2 fixes the content and order of their defects; L-theories-3 determines class names; L-theories-4 constructs the colimit and its provenance; L-theories-5 shows that a composition’s result is well formed and well typed; L-theories-6 specifies the union-find closure; L-theories-7 checks theory morphisms; and L-theories-8 replays recorded compositions. Pushouts and merge develops the construction, using the conventions of Shared notation.

A theory has sorts, operations, equations, directed equations and conflict policies. A sort has a name, a telescope of parameters (p1​:P1​,…,pn​:Pn​), a kind, and either no constructor list (an open sort) or a list of constructor names (a closed sort). An operation has a name, a telescope of inputs (x1​:S1​,…,xn​:Sn​), each marked explicit or implicit, and an output sort S. A sort expression is a sort name s, or a sort applied to argument terms s(t1​,…,tk​); its head is s. In name-free comparisons (the class checks of a composition and the checks of a morphism) s() and s are one sort expression; signature checking reads s() as an application with no arguments, so s() of a sort with parameters is a SortArity defect. A term is a variable, an application o(t1​,…,tk​) of an operation name, a hole, a local binding let x=t in u, or a case analysis whose branches bind one name per input of their constructor. A telescope’s binders are the names pi​ (or xi​); the binders before position i are the context of position i.

Several blocks compare sort expressions through their name-free forms. Given a list of binder names β, the name-free form nfβ​(t) of a term t replaces each variable x by the position of the last occurrence of x in β, or by the marker free(x) when β does not contain x; it erases the names a local binding or a case branch binds, extending β by them inside their scope; and it keeps operation names, holes and the shape of the term. The name-free form of a sort expression applies nfβ​ to each argument and keeps the head. Two sort expressions read under β and β′ are equal up to binder names when their name-free forms under β and β′ are equal. Inside a composition the same forms are taken with every sort and operation name replaced by its class (L-theories-4); a variable’s position is read as above.

Equations are compared through the joint name-free form of their two sides: the free variables of l and then of r are listed in order of first occurrence, a binder’s own occurrences in its scope left out, and both sides are put in name-free form under that one list. Two equations l=r and l′=r′ are alpha-equivalent when their joint name-free forms are equal, that is when one bijective renaming of free variables, applied to both sides at once, carries one to the other up to the names of local binders; thus x = x and y = y are alpha-equivalent, while x = y and x = x are not. Two terms l and r agree in one context when the two sides of their joint name-free form are equal: local binders are read by position, and a free variable is held fixed by name, so the free variables x and y never agree.

The depth of a term is one for a variable, a hole and an application with no arguments, and one more than the deepest argument (or bound term, body, scrutinee or branch body) otherwise. The executable counterparts below use the two-sort alphabet of the enumerator panproto_reference::enumerate::theories: sorts A (open or closed over the operations whose output is A, of kind structural or, when it is the only sort, of kind value) and B (simple, or B(p : A)); at most two operations f and g of arity at most two, with inputs x and y, each input and output of sort A, a simple B, or the dependent B applied to an earlier input of sort A; and at most one equation e between terms of depth at most one, that is between the variables x, y and the constants.

A signature passes checking exactly when it is well formed

Statement

For every theory T, typecheck_signature(T) returns Ok(()) exactly when T is well formed, that is when all of the following hold.

  1. No two sorts, no two operations, no two equations, no two directed equations and no two policies of T share a name.
  2. No telescope of T (the parameters of a sort, the inputs of an operation) binds one name twice.
  3. Every sort parameter’s sort Pi​ has a head that T declares, and that head belongs to the dependency order D(T).
  4. Every sort expression of T (each parameter sort whose head lies in D(T), each input sort and each output sort) is well formed at its site in the context of the binders before it.

Here D(T) is the least set of sort names such that a declared sort s belongs to it whenever every parameter sort of s has a head h=s that either no sort of T declares or that already belongs to the set. A sort expression E is well formed at a site in a context Γ when its head names a declared sort s (the last declaration of that name) and, if E is an application s(t1​,…,tk​), then k is the number of parameters of s, every variable of each ti​ is bound in Γ, and each ti​ has, in Γ, a sort equal (up to the names of local binders) to the parameter sort Pi​ with p1​,…,pi−1​ replaced by t1​,…,ti−1​. A sort name s written without arguments is well formed whenever s is declared, whatever the number of its parameters: it is the unapplied, implicitly indexed form.

Outside the domain

There is none: the statement holds for every theory. A theory that is not well formed is refused with the non-empty list of its defects, whose exact content and order are the subject of L-theories-2.

Definitions used

The theory, telescope, context, sort expression and head are defined at the top of this page. The sort of a term in a context is the one the typing judgement of the theory assigns: a variable has the sort its last binding in the context gives it; an application o(u1​,…,um​) of an operation whose explicit arity is m has the operation’s output sort with its inputs replaced by the arguments (implicit inputs inferred by first-order unification); a hole has a sort of its own; a local binding has the sort of its body; a case analysis over a closed sort has the common sort of its branches. A term has no sort when this judgement fails, and then ti​ is not of the parameter sort.

Proof

  1. typecheck_signature builds one list of defects and returns Ok(()) exactly when the list is empty (definition of typecheck_signature).
  2. The list receives a DuplicateSymbol defect for each name that occurs twice or more among the sorts, the operations, the equations, the directed equations or the policies, and no other defect from this pass. So this pass adds nothing exactly when condition 1 holds.
  3. For every parameter of every sort, and every input of every operation, the list receives a DuplicateBinder defect exactly when the binder’s name occurs earlier in the same telescope. So these defects are absent exactly when condition 2 holds.
  4. For a sort parameter whose sort’s head h is declared but does not belong to D(T), the list receives UndeclaredSort for h. The procedure computes D(T) by adding, round by round, every declared sort whose parameter heads are all different from it and either undeclared or already added, and stops when a round adds nothing; this is the least fixed point of the definition in the Statement, because each added sort satisfies the defining condition with respect to the sorts added before it, and a sort satisfying the condition with respect to the final set would have been added in the last round.
  5. Every other sort expression is checked at its site with the context of the binders before it. The check adds UndeclaredSort when no sort of that name is declared and stops; otherwise, for an application whose argument count differs from the number of parameters, it adds SortArity and stops; otherwise, for each argument in order, it adds one UnboundParamVar per variable that the context does not bind, and, when there is none, adds ParamIllTyped when the argument has no sort or a sort different from the instantiated parameter sort. A sort name without arguments adds nothing once its head is declared. So the check adds nothing exactly when the expression is well formed at its site.
  6. A parameter sort whose head is undeclared falls under step 5, which adds UndeclaredSort for it; one whose head is declared and outside D(T) falls under step 4. Together, steps 4 and 5 add nothing exactly when conditions 3 and 4 hold.
  7. By steps 2, 3 and 6, the list is empty exactly when conditions 1 to 4 hold, and by step 1 this is when the result is Ok(()).

Signature checking reports every defect once, in a fixed order

Statement

For every theory T that is not well formed (L-theories-1), typecheck_signature(T) returns Err with the following list, and only it, in this order.

  1. For each kind in the order sort, operation, equation, directed equation, policy, one DuplicateSymbol(kind, name) for each name that two or more symbols of that kind share, the names in ascending byte order.
  2. For each sort in declaration order and each of its parameters pi​:Pi​ in order, at the site SortParam(sort, i): first DuplicateBinder(site, p_i) when pi​ is among p1​,…,pi−1​; then UndeclaredSort(site, h) when the head h of Pi​ is declared and outside D(T), and otherwise the expression defects of Pi​ in the context p1​:P1​,…,pi−1​:Pi−1​.
  3. For each operation in declaration order and each of its inputs xi​:Si​ in order, at the site OpInput(op, i): first DuplicateBinder(site, x_i) when xi​ is among x1​,…,xi−1​; then the expression defects of Si​ in the context of x1​,…,xi−1​. Then, at OpOutput(op), the expression defects of the output sort in the context of all the inputs.

The expression defects of a sort expression E at a site in a context Γ are: UndeclaredSort(site, h) alone when no sort of E’s head h is declared; nothing more when E is a sort name; SortArity(site, s, n, k) alone when E=s(t1​,…,tk​) and the last declaration of s has n=k parameters; and otherwise, for each argument ti​ in order, one UnboundParamVar(site, v) for each free variable v of ti​ that Γ does not bind, in ascending byte order of v, or, when there is none, ParamIllTyped(site, s, i, expected, found) when ti​ has no sort in Γ (with found reporting the failure) or a sort that differs, up to the names of local binders, from the parameter sort Pi​ with p1​,…,pi−1​ replaced by t1​,…,ti−1​ (expected and found being those two sorts as written).

Outside the domain

For a well-formed theory the result is Ok(()) (L-theories-1); there is no list. No other input is outside the domain.

Definitions used

D(T), well-formedness at a site, the sort of a term and the context are those of L-theories-1. The sites SortParam(sort, i), OpInput(op, i) and OpOutput(op) name the parameter, input and output of the named declaration. The byte order of names is the lexicographic order of their UTF-8 bytes.

Proof

  1. typecheck_signature first computes the duplicate-name defects, then walks the sorts in declaration order, then the operations in declaration order, appending to one list, and returns the list when it is not empty (definition). So the list is the concatenation of the three parts of the Statement in that order, provided each part is as stated.
  2. Part 1. For each kind in the stated order, the procedure scans the names of that kind and collects, into a set ordered by bytes, every name seen before; it then appends one DuplicateSymbol per collected name in that order. A name shared by m≥2 symbols is collected (at its second occurrence) and, being in a set, appears once.
  3. Part 2. For the parameter i of a sort the procedure first tests whether pi​ is among the earlier parameter names and appends DuplicateBinder if so; it then appends UndeclaredSort when the head is declared and outside D(T), and otherwise runs the expression check with the earlier parameters as context. These are the stated defects in the stated order.
  4. Part 3. For the input i of an operation the procedure appends DuplicateBinder when xi​ is among the earlier input names, then runs the expression check with the earlier inputs as context; after the inputs it runs the expression check on the output with all inputs as context.
  5. The expression check, at a site with a context, looks up the last declaration of the head. Without one it appends UndeclaredSort and returns. A sort name returns with nothing more. An application with an argument count different from the declared parameter count appends SortArity with the declared and given counts and returns. Otherwise, for each argument in order it collects the argument’s variables that the context does not bind, as a set ordered by bytes; when the set is not empty it appends one UnboundParamVar per member and goes to the next argument; when it is empty it computes the instantiated parameter sort and the argument’s sort and appends ParamIllTyped when inference fails or the two sorts differ. These are the expression defects as defined.
  6. By steps 2 to 5 each part is as stated, and by step 1 the list is their concatenation. Each defect is appended at most once at its site, so each is reported once.

Classes are named by their majority name, and freshened names never collide

Statement

Let D be a diagram and O composition options such that compose(D,O) returns a composition C, and fix one symbol kind. For a class K of that kind (a sort or operation class, or an equation, directed-equation or policy class), write cand(K) for its candidate: the name that occurs most often among the names of K’s members, ties going to the least such name by bytes. Write N(K) for the name C gives K. Then:

  1. cand(K) depends only on the multiset of K’s member names.
  2. Under Amalgamate, N(K)=cand(K) for every sort and operation class K.
  3. Under ExplicitIdentity with Reject, N(K)=cand(K) for every class K, and no two classes of one kind share a candidate.
  4. Under ExplicitIdentity with Freshen, the names N(K) of the classes of one kind are pairwise distinct and differ from every reserved name; a class whose candidate no other class of its kind shares and that is not reserved keeps N(K)=cand(K) unless a later pass finds cand(K) shared, in which case N(K) is cand(K) followed by suffixes #k; every class whose candidate is shared or reserved gets ℓ.cand(K), with ℓ the least node label among its members, possibly followed by suffixes #k.
  5. report.freshened lists, for exactly the classes K with N(K)=cand(K), the members of K, cand(K) and N(K).

The reserved names are those of the generated equations of L-theories-4, and only equation classes have them.

Outside the domain

When compose refuses, there is no composition to name. The refusals that belong to naming report the least clashing name in byte order: under ExplicitIdentity with Reject, NameClash(kind, name, classes) when two or more classes of one kind share the candidate name, or one class’s candidate is a reserved name, classes being every such class (and the reserved name’s holder as a one-member class), ascending; and under Amalgamate, NameConflict(kind, name, first, second) when an equation class’s candidate is a reserved name, first being the class’s least member and second the holder. The remaining refusals are those of L-theories-4.

Definitions used

The classes, the node labels and the generated equations are those of L-theories-4. The suffixing rule is: while some name is shared by two or more classes of one kind, or equals a reserved name, every class holding such a name n is renamed n#k, where k is the class’s position, counted from one, among the classes holding n in ascending order of their sorted member lists. A pass is one application of this rule to all such names at once.

Proof

  1. The candidate is computed from the count of each member name and the byte order of names, both functions of the multiset of names; this gives part 1.
  2. Under Amalgamate, the composition joins every two same-kind symbols of different nodes that share a name, so the members of distinct sort or operation classes have disjoint name sets. The candidate of a class is one of its members’ names, so two distinct classes cannot share a candidate, no clash arises, and N(K)=cand(K); this gives part 2.
  3. Under ExplicitIdentity with Reject, naming refuses with NameClash as soon as a candidate is shared or reserved. Since C was returned, no candidate is shared or reserved, no class is renamed, and N(K)=cand(K); this gives part 3.
  4. Under ExplicitIdentity with Freshen, naming first renames every class whose candidate is shared or reserved to ℓ.cand(K) and leaves the others with their candidates, then applies the suffixing rule until no name is shared or reserved. When it stops, the names of one kind are pairwise distinct and none is reserved, by its stopping condition. It remains to show that it stops.
  5. Each pass renames a class K holding n to n#k, which extends K’s name, so every class’s name only grows by appended components #k (with k a decimal numeral, which contains no #).
  6. Two classes that hold one name n in some pass both take part in that pass and are renamed to n#a and n#b with a=b. From then on their names are n#aα and n#bβ, with α and β sequences of components. Both start with n, and the rest of each splits uniquely at its # characters into components, the first being a and b respectively; so the two names differ in every later pass, and the two classes share a name in at most one pass.
  7. A class holding a reserved name r in some pass is renamed to a longer name, and by step 5 never again holds r. So each class holds each reserved name in at most one pass.
  8. A pass happens only when some name is shared by two classes or equals a reserved name. By steps 6 and 7, the number of passes is at most the number of pairs of classes of the kind plus the number of classes times the number of reserved names, a finite number. So naming stops, and part 4 follows from step 4.
  9. Every renaming in steps 3 and 4 records one freshening with the class’s members, its candidate and its final name exactly when the final name differs from the candidate, and no other entry is recorded; this gives part 5.

The colimit identifies exactly what the diagram relates, with total provenance and a commuting cocone

Statement

Let D be a diagram with nodes n1​,…,nm​ (labels and theories Tni​​) and edges e1​,…,eq​ (each a morphism from the theory of its source node to that of its target node), and let O be composition options. Suppose compose(D,O) returns a composition C with result theory R. Then:

  1. The sort classes and the operation classes of C are exactly the equivalence classes of the relation ∼ defined below, and each class K has the name N(K) of L-theories-3.
  2. R declares one sort for each sort class with a declared member, namely the declaration of the class’s least declared member with every sort and operation name replaced by its class’s name, and one operation for each operation class, built in the same way from its least member. The sort classes with no declared member are the free names of C; R does not declare them.
  3. R’s equations are one representative per equation class (the least member’s translated equation, named by L-theories-3) together with every generated equation; its directed equations and policies are one representative per class. Two translated equations have equal content when they are alpha-equivalent in either orientation; two translated directed equations when they are alpha-equivalent in the stated orientation and have the same implementation, inverse, value kinds and classification; two policies when they have the same value kind and strategy. In both modes an element joins the class of equal content, whatever its name.
  4. The provenance is total and exact: every sort symbol (declared or used without declaration), operation, equation, directed equation and policy of every node has exactly one image, the name of its class; added_eqs maps each generated equation’s name to its edge’s position k in the canonical edge order and the operation it identifies.
  5. For each node ni​, inclusions[i] sends every sort symbol and every operation of ni​ to its class’s name.
  6. The cocone commutes: for every edge e:s→t, every sort assignment a↦b of e between symbols of s and t gives s.a and t.b one image, and every operation assignment o↦o′ by name gives s.o and t.o′ one image; for every assignment of o to a term, R holds the generated equation that equates N([s.o]) applied to o’s explicit inputs with that term in result names.
  7. Every inclusion is a theory morphism: check_morphism(Tni​​,R,inclusions[i]) returns Ok(()).
  8. The report lists, sorted by check and subject, NodeWellFormed, InclusionIsMorphism and EquationImage for each node, LegIsMorphism and CoconeCommutes for each edge, and ClassSignaturesAgree, UniversalIdentity, ResultWellTyped and NameClashFree for the diagram. CoconeCommutes for an edge is PassedByEquation with the least name of the equations that edge generated, and Passed when it generated none. InclusionIsMorphism for a node, and UniversalIdentity, are Inapplicable with reason OpenTheory listing the free names involved (ascending) when there are any, and Passed otherwise. Every other record is Passed. The status is Failed when a record failed, else Incomplete when one is inapplicable or incomplete, else Passed; name_clash_free holds exactly when every two same-kind, same-name symbols of different nodes are related by ∼E​ and every two same-name equations (directed equations, policies) of different nodes have one image.

The relation ∼E​ on symbols of one kind is the least equivalence relation containing: s.a∼E​t.b for every edge e:s→t and sort assignment a↦b of e such that a is a sort symbol of s and b one of t; s.o∼E​t.o′ for every operation assignment o↦o′ by name such that o and o′ are operations of s and t; and, for every sort name u some node uses without declaring, n.u∼E​d for every such use n.u and every declared sort d named u, or, when no node declares u, n.u∼E​n′.u for every two such uses. The relation ∼ is ∼E​ under ExplicitIdentity, and under Amalgamate the least equivalence relation containing ∼E​ and every pair of same-kind, same-name symbols of different nodes.

Outside the domain

compose refuses, with the first of these in this order.

  1. InvalidNodeLabel(label) for the first node, in diagram order, whose label is empty or contains . or #; DuplicateNodeLabel(label) for the first label met twice (checked node by node, the label test first).
  2. UnknownNodeLabel(label) for the first edge, in order, whose source or (then) target label names no node.
  3. InvalidNode(label, defects) for the first node whose signature defects (L-theories-2) include any other than UndeclaredSort of a name the node does not declare; defects are those.
  4. InvalidLeg { edge, detail } for the first edge that is not a theory morphism (L-theories-7), with that check’s refusal in its stated order (so a leg sending an operation to one its target lacks reports OpNotFound before any unmapped head of the operation’s signature, and a leg sending an operation to an ill-formed term reports the first defect the typing of that term meets), or whose morphism sends a sort name its source uses without declaring to a name its target neither declares nor uses (a detail naming the unknown image; the reference names this refusal ImplicitSortImageUnknown).
  5. Under ExplicitIdentity, NameClash(Sort, u, classes) for the first sort name u (ascending) used without declaration whose declarations lie in two or more classes of ∼E​ without the uses, classes being those classes.
  6. For the first sort class, then the first operation class, in ascending order of member lists, whose members disagree: with first its least declared member and second the least declared member that disagrees with it, or, when none does and first has parameters, the least member used without declaration, NameConflict(kind, name of first, first, second) under Amalgamate when the class is formed by the name rule alone (no two of its members are related by ∼E​), and otherwise SortSignatureMismatch(first, second, detail) (the first difference in the order arity, parameter sort by position, kind, closure, a used but undeclared member counting as arity zero) for sorts, and for operations QuotientNotRepresentable { first, second, NeedsSortEquation } when the two signatures have the same number of inputs, the same implicit flags, and inputs and outputs with the same head classes and the same numbers of sort arguments (so that only their term arguments differ), and OpSignatureMismatch(first, second) otherwise.
  7. The naming refusals of L-theories-3 for sort and operation classes.
  8. IllTypedTransport { source, .. } for the first translated equation, in the order: node equations (nodes in diagram order), generated equations (in canonical edge order, operations ascending, source being the operation), directed equations (nodes in diagram order), that does not typecheck against R’s sorts and operations.
  9. Under Amalgamate, for equations, then directed equations, then policies, NameConflict(kind, name, first, second) when one name is carried by members of two classes: reading the classes in ascending order of member lists and each class’s members in ascending order, the first member whose name a member of an earlier class carries, first and second being that member and the first member read with its name, in ascending order; then the naming refusals of L-theories-3 for these classes.
  10. The refusals of L-theories-5.

Definitions used

A diagram, its labels and composition options are as in TheoryDiagram and ComposeOptions. A symbol is a pair of a node label and a name; the sort symbols of a node are its declared sorts and the sort names its signatures use without declaring. Two members of a sort class disagree when their signatures differ once every sort and operation name is replaced by its class: arity, the name-free form of each parameter sort, kind, and the set of constructor classes; two members of an operation class disagree when their name-free signatures with implicit flags differ in the same sense. The canonical edge order sorts the edges by source label, target label and the canonical bytes of the morphism. A generated equation is the equation N([s.o])(x1​,…,xr​)=t′ named {s}.{o}~{k}, for each edge e:s→t at position k of the canonical edge order and each operation o that e assigns to a term t, the xi​ being o’s explicit inputs and t′ being t with each operation renamed to its class’s name in t’s node. The least member of a class is the least in the order of (label, name). Theory morphisms and check_morphism are those of L-theories-7; the union-find closure is that of L-theories-6.

Proof

  1. The composition creates one element per sort symbol (declared or used without declaration) and per operation of every node, then joins, for each edge in order, every sort assignment and every operation assignment by name whose two symbols exist, then joins the uses of each undeclared name as the definition of ∼E​ says. Each join merges the two current classes, so after these joins the classes are those of the least equivalence relation containing the joined pairs (L-theories-6, whose argument holds for any finite list of joins). These are the classes of ∼E​.
  2. Under Amalgamate it then joins every two same-kind, same-name symbols of different nodes, and by the same argument the classes become those of ∼; under ExplicitIdentity it joins nothing more and ∼=∼E​. Naming gives each class N(K) (L-theories-3). This proves part 1.
  3. The result’s sorts are built class by class from the least declared member’s declaration, every sort and operation name replaced by the class name of its node’s symbol, and a class with no declared member contributes no sort and is recorded as free; its operations are built in the same way from each class’s least member. This proves part 2.
  4. Every node equation, translated into result names, is classified as the Statement says, and each class contributes its least member’s translation under its name; every generated equation is added under its own name and is not classified with the node equations. A composition’s result is a well-formed, well-typed theory

Statement

Let D be a diagram and O composition options such that compose(D,O) returns a composition C with result theory R and free names F (L-theories-4). Then:

  1. every defect typecheck_signature(R) reports is UndeclaredSort(site, u) with u∈F; in particular, when F is empty, R is well formed (L-theories-1);
  2. every equation and every directed equation of R is well typed in R: its two sides have one sort under the sorts its variables receive from their positions as operation arguments;
  3. R passes the remaining checks of the theory body: every implicit input of every operation occurs in the sort of an explicit input or in the output sort, and every closed sort’s constructors are operations of that sort, every other operation of that sort being defined by an equation whose left side applies it to distinct variables;
  4. R’s name is O.result_name, its extends is empty, and its sorts, operations, equations, directed equations and policies are each in ascending name order.

Outside the domain

compose refuses with IllTypedResult(defects) when R’s signature has a defect other than UndeclaredSort of a free name, defects being those; and with IllTypedResult([]) when the signature passes but a check of part 3 fails. These come after every refusal of L-theories-4. A translated equation that is not well typed is refused earlier, with IllTypedTransport (L-theories-4, refusal 8).

Definitions used

Well-formedness and the defects are those of L-theories-1 and L-theories-2; free names, the classes and the generated equations are those of L-theories-4. An equation is well typed in a theory when, after each free variable receives the sort required at the operation-argument positions where it occurs (equal sorts at all of them, with implicit inputs solved by first-order unification), both sides have a sort and the two sorts are equal.

Proof

  1. After the result is assembled, the composition runs typecheck_signature on R, discards the UndeclaredSort defects whose sort is a free name, and refuses with IllTypedResult if any defect remains. Since C was returned, none remained; this gives part 1, and when F is empty there is no defect at all, so R is well formed by L-theories-1.
  2. Before classifying equations, the composition checks every translated node equation, every generated equation and every translated directed equation (as the equation between its two sides) for well-typedness against R’s sorts and operations, and refuses with IllTypedTransport at the first that fails. Since C was returned, all of them are well typed. Each equation of R is either a class representative, which is one of the translated node equations, or a generated equation; each directed equation of R is a class representative, one of the translated directed equations. Well-typedness depends only on the sorts and operations of R, which do not change after this check. This gives part 2.
  3. Last, the composition runs the remaining theory-body checks on R and refuses with IllTypedResult([]) if one fails. Since C was returned, all passed; this gives part 3.
  4. The composition names R by O.result_name, leaves extends empty, sorts the sorts and operations by name, and the classification yields equations, directed equations and policies in ascending name order, the generated equations being added and the whole list sorted again. This gives part 4.

The union-find closure labels each element by the least member of its class

Statement

  1. Union-find. Let K be a totally ordered set of keys and u a production UnionFind over K that has received a finite sequence of insert, find and union calls. Write ≡u​ for the least equivalence relation on the keys u holds that contains every pair passed to union. Then find(k) returns the least key of k’s ≡u​ class (inserting k as a singleton first when u does not hold it); union(a,b) returns true exactly when a and b were in distinct classes before the call; classes() maps the least key of each class to the class’s keys in ascending order; and rename_map() maps every key that is not the least of its class to that least key.
  2. Indexed quotient. For every size n and every finite list I=((l1​,r1​),…,(lm​,rm​)) of pairs of naturals, write ≡I​ for the least equivalence relation on {0,…,n−1} containing every pair of I and λ(i)=min{j<n:j≡I​i}. The reference quotient(n,I) returns None when some lj​ or rj​ is at least n and Some(λ) otherwise. For n≤10, the production quotient of the indexed theory Tn​ by the pairs (slj​​,srj​​) refuses with QuotientIncompatible (the names are not both sorts of the theory) in the first case, and otherwise returns a theory in which each constant ci​ has the output sort sλ(i)​.

Outside the domain

There is none: the statement covers every sequence of calls, every size and every list, the out-of-range case included.

Definitions used

An equivalence relation is reflexive, symmetric and transitive; the least one containing a set of pairs is the intersection of all that contain it, and its classes partition the keys. A UnionFind holds a parent map on its keys; a root is a key that is its own parent, and the tree of a root is the set of keys whose chain of parents reaches it. The indexed theory Tn​ declares the simple sorts s0​,…,sn−1​ and the constants ci​:si​; for n≤10 the byte order of the names si​ is the order of the indices.

Proof

  1. Invariant of the union-find. We show that after every call: (a) every key’s parent is a key of its own ≡u​ class and no greater than the key; (b) two keys lie in one tree exactly when they are ≡u​-related. Initially u is empty and both hold vacuously.
  2. insert(k) adds k as its own parent when it is new, a new singleton class and a new singleton tree; (a) and (b) are kept.
  3. find(k) inserts k, follows parents from k to the root ρ of its tree, then sets the parent of every key on that path to ρ. By (a), each step of the chain moves to a key of the same class that is no greater, and the chain stops at a key that is its own parent, so ρ is in k’s class and ρ≤ every key on the path. Redirecting a parent to ρ keeps (a), and it moves no key out of its tree, keeping (b).
  4. union(a,b) computes the roots ρa​= find(a) and ρb​= find(b). When they are equal, a≡u​b already by (b), the relation does not change, nothing is written and the call returns false. Otherwise a and b lie in distinct classes by (b); the call sets the parent of the greater root to the lesser and returns true. The new relation merges exactly the classes of a and b, and the write merges exactly their trees, keeping (b); the new parent is in the merged class and less than the key it is written for, keeping (a).
  5. Roots are least. Every key of a tree reaches its root by a chain that, by (a), never increases, so the root is no greater than any key of its tree; by (b) the tree is the key’s class. So find(k) returns the least key of k’s class, which proves the claim on find; step 4 proves the claim on union; and classes and rename_map, which call find on every key and group or compare the results, report the stated maps.
  6. Reference quotient. If some pair of I has a member at least n, the reference returns None when it reaches the first such pair. Otherwise it starts from the labels λ0​(i)=i and, for each pair (l,r) in turn, with a and b the current labels of l and r, sets to min(a,b) every label equal to a or b. By induction on the pairs, as in steps 4 and 5, equal labels are exactly related indices and each label is the least index of its class; after the last pair this is λ.
  7. Production quotient. The production quotient refuses with QuotientIncompatible when a pair names something that is not a sort of Tn​, which happens exactly when an index is at least n. Otherwise it unions the named sorts in a UnionFind, renames every sort to its class’s least name by rename_map (step 5), and rebuilds Tn​, so ci​’s output sort is the least sj​ with j≡I​i, that is sλ(i)​, since the byte order of the names is the order of the indices for n≤10.

A morphism check accepts exactly the theory morphisms, and reports the first failure

Statement

Let S and T be theories and m a pair of maps, msort​ from sort names to sort names and mop​ from operation names to images (an operation name, or a term). Write m^ for the whole action of m on terms of S: it renames an application of an operation sent to an operation, replaces an application of an operation o sent to a term t by t with o’s inputs replaced, without capture, by the translated arguments, replaces each case branch’s constructor by its constructor image (keeping it when there is none), and keeps variables, holes and binders. Write F^ for its action on sort expressions: the head goes through msort​ (kept when unmapped) and each argument through m^. Then check_morphism(S,T,m) returns Ok(()) exactly when m is a theory morphism from S to T:

  1. for every sort s of S, in declaration order: msort​(s) is defined and names a sort s′ of T (its last declaration) with as many parameters as s and the same kind; for each parameter i, F^(Pi​) read under s’s parameters before i equals s′’s parameter sort i read under s′’s parameters before i, up to binder names; and if s is closed, the set of constructor images of s’s constructors equals the set of s′’s constructors (empty when s′ is open);
  2. for every operation o:(x1​:S1​,…,xn​:Sn​)→S0​ of S, in declaration order: mop​(o) is defined, and either mop​(o) names an operation o′ of T with n inputs such that, for each input i in order, msort​ is defined on the head of Si​ and o′’s input sort i equals F^(Si​) up to binder names (each read under the inputs before it), and then msort​ is defined on the head of S0​ and o′’s output sort equals F^(S0​) up to binder names (read under all the inputs); or mop​(o) is a term t such that msort​ is defined on the head of every Si​, t has a sort in T under the context x1​:F^(S1​),…,xn​:F^(Sn​) (so every free variable of t is one of the xi​), msort​ is defined on the head of S0​, and the sort of t equals F^(S0​) up to the names of local binders;
  3. for every equation l=r of S, in order, either some equation of T is alpha-equivalent to m^(l)=m^(r) or to m^(r)=m^(l), or m^(l) and m^(r) agree in one context, or the two sides are shown equal in T within 512 rewrite steps for each side: first by the directed equations of T alone, then by those and every equation of T in both orientations;
  4. for every directed equation l→r of S, in order, some directed equation of T is alpha-equivalent to m^(l)→m^(r) in the stated orientation.

When m is not a theory morphism, check_morphism returns the refusal of the first failed condition in the order of the clauses above, each clause’s conditions in the order written: MissingSortMapping, SortNotFound, SortArityMismatch, SortKindMismatch, SortParamMismatch (the first parameter), ClosureMismatch; MissingOpMapping; then, for an operation image, OpNotFound when T declares no operation of that name, OpTypeMismatch when the input counts differ, then for each input in order MissingSortMapping when its head is unmapped and OpTypeMismatch when its sort differs, then MissingSortMapping for the output’s head and OpTypeMismatch for the output’s sort; for a term image, MissingSortMapping at the first input whose head is unmapped, then the first defect the typing judgement of T meets in t under the translated context, reported as TermAssignmentUnboundVar when that defect is a variable outside the context and as TermAssignmentIllTyped otherwise, then MissingSortMapping for the output’s head, then TermAssignmentSortMismatch; EquationNotPreserved when rewriting reaches normal forms that differ, EquationPreservationUnknown when it exhausts its budget; DirectedEquationNotPreserved.

Outside the domain

There is none: every pair of theories and every pair of maps is in the domain, and every refusal is listed in the Statement. The equation clause is a bounded decision: an equation derivable in T only beyond the step budget, or only by a rewrite sequence the procedure does not follow, is refused with EquationPreservationUnknown or EquationNotPreserved; the check never accepts an equation it has not shown preserved.

Definitions used

Equality up to binder names, name-free forms and the sort of a term are defined at the top of this page and in L-theories-1. The typing judgement meets the defects of a term in this order: a variable is looked up in the context; an application looks up its operation, then compares the argument count, then types the arguments left to right, comparing each argument’s sort with the expected one as soon as it is typed, except that, for an operation with implicit inputs, only the heads are compared then and the implicit inputs are solved by unification after every argument is typed; a let types its bound term and then its body; a case types its scrutinee, then checks that the scrutinee’s sort is declared and closed and that the branches are exhaustive and not redundant, then types each branch body in order. The constructor image of a constructor c of S is k when mop​(c) is the operation k, c itself when mop​(c) is undefined, and k when mop​(c) is the term k(x1​,…,xn​) whose i-th argument is a variable denoting c’s i-th input (the last input of that name); otherwise c has none, and the set compared contains that term, written out, in its place. Alpha-equivalence of equations and agreement in one context are defined at the top of this page; the shown equality of two sides is agreement in one context of their normal forms. Rewriting a term with a list of rules applies, at each node after its children are normal, the first rule whose left side matches, counting each application as one step, and gives up when the budget is spent or the term grows more than 64 levels deeper than every rule side and the starting term.

Proof

  1. check_morphism runs the sort pass, then the operation pass, then the equation pass, then the directed-equation pass, and returns the first refusal any of them returns, or Ok(()) when none does (definition).
  2. The sort pass visits the sorts of S in declaration order and, for each, refuses with MissingSortMapping when msort​ is undefined, SortNotFound when T declares no sort of the image’s name, SortArityMismatch and SortKindMismatch when arity or kind differ, SortParamMismatch at the first parameter whose translated name-free form differs from the target’s, and ClosureMismatch when a closed sort’s constructor image set (constructors without an image represented by their rendered term) differs from the target’s constructor set. So it returns nothing exactly when clause 1 holds, and otherwise the first failure in the stated order.
  3. The operation pass visits the operations in declaration order and, for each, refuses with MissingOpMapping when mop​ is undefined. For an operation image it refuses with OpNotFound when T declares no operation of that name, and with OpTypeMismatch when the input counts differ; it then visits the inputs in order, refusing with MissingSortMapping when the input’s head is unmapped and with OpTypeMismatch when F^ of its sort and the target’s input sort have different name-free forms, and finally does the same for the output. For a term image it refuses with MissingSortMapping at the first input whose head is unmapped, then types t in T under the context binding each xi​ to F^(Si​) and, when typing fails, refuses with TermAssignmentUnboundVar naming the variable if the first defect it meets is a variable outside the context, and with TermAssignmentIllTyped otherwise; it then refuses with MissingSortMapping when the output’s head is unmapped and with TermAssignmentSortMismatch when the sort of t differs from F^(S0​) in name-free form under the inputs (the same binder list on both sides, so up to local binder names). Each refusal is the failure of the condition of clause 2 checked at that point, in the order clause 2 states them. So it returns nothing exactly when clause 2 holds.
  4. The equation pass translates each equation’s sides by m^ and accepts it when some equation of T is alpha-equivalent to it in either orientation. Otherwise it accepts when the two sides agree in one context, or when their normal forms under the directed equations agree, or when their normal forms under the directed equations and every equation in both orientations agree; it refuses with EquationPreservationUnknown when a normalization exhausts its budget before deciding, and with EquationNotPreserved when both normalizations end and differ. So it returns nothing exactly when clause 3 holds.
  5. The directed-equation pass accepts a directed equation exactly when T holds one alpha-equivalent to its image in the same orientation, and refuses with DirectedEquationNotPreserved otherwise; this is clause 4.
  6. By steps 1 to 5, check_morphism returns Ok(()) exactly when clauses 1 to 4 hold, and otherwise the first failure in the stated order.

Replaying a recipe composes each step from its recorded data

Statement

Let Σ be a recipe with result name ρ and steps σ0​,…,σk−1​, and M0​ a registry of named theories. Define the registries Mj+1​ and the names νj+1​ by replaying σj​ against Mj​:

  1. Base(n): n is a key of Mj​; Mj+1​=Mj​ and νj+1​=n.
  2. Colimit { left, right, shared_sorts, shared_ops, mode }: left and right are keys of Mj​; the interface I holds, in order, the declaration in Mj​(left) of each shared sort and then of each shared operation, each of which both Mj​(left) and Mj​(right) declare; the composition of the by-name span left←I→right, with the interface labeled step_j_interface and the two sides labeled by their names, under mode, Reject, result name step_j and rewrite budget 512, returns a composition; Mj+1​ adds its theory under step_j, and νj+1​ = step_j.
  3. Diagram { nodes, edges, mode, clash, rewrite_budget, inline }: no two theories of inline have the same name; each node name resolves to the inline theory of that name or, failing that, to Mj​’s; the diagram of those nodes, labeled by their names, and of one edge per recorded edge (its maps as recorded) composes under the recorded options with result name step_j; Mj+1​ adds its theory under step_j, and νj+1​ = step_j.

Then recompose(Σ,M0​) returns Mk​(νk​) renamed ρ when k≥1 and every step replays.

Outside the domain

recompose refuses at the first step that does not replay, in step order: DuplicateInlineTheory { step, name } for a diagram step two of whose inline theories have the same name, name being that of the first inline theory, in order, whose name an earlier one carries, before any node name of the step is resolved; TheoryNotFound(n) for a base step whose name, a colimit step whose left (then right), or, in a diagram step, the first node in order, is neither inline nor registered; FactorizationError naming n (the reference’s MissingShared(n)) for the first shared sort, then the first shared operation, that one side does not declare; and Composition(e) when the step’s composition refuses with e (L-theories-4). An empty recipe refuses with TheoryNotFound, since it has no last step.

Definitions used

The by-name span, its legs (the identity on the interface’s sort and operation names, and on the sort names the interface uses without declaring), compositions, modes and clash policies are those of L-theories-4 and L-theories-3.

Proof

  1. recompose keeps a registry, initially M0​, and for each step in order replays it with the name step_j: a base step checks that its name is registered and records it as the last name; a colimit or diagram step computes a theory, adds it under step_j and records step_j as the last name (definition). By induction on j, the registry and last name after step j are Mj+1​ and νj+1​, provided each step replays, and the first step that does not replay ends the replay with its refusal.
  2. This is clause 3 and its refusals.
